# Five-Minute Initial Thesis Presentation

## Format

Use **five image-led slides plus one physical Power Mesh sample**. One slide equals one minute. The presentation is an argument, not a compressed research report.

## Core sentence

> I am investigating whether a variable-tension textile boundary can learn from movement patterns and make gradual spatial proposals while occupants retain the ability to understand, reject, and reshape them.

### 中文思考笔记

一句话顺序是：问题 → boundary → agent → 人的主动权。不要从“我选了一块布”开始。

## Slide 1 — The problem (0:00–0:45)

**Show:** one plan/section illustrating a compact space changing between solitude, co-presence, and social use.  
**Say:** fixed boundaries support one configuration, but relationships and activities change. The research problem is how space can adapt without deciding for people.

## Slide 2 — The thesis mechanism (0:45–1:30)

**Show:** one loop diagram:

```text
trajectory → interpretation with uncertainty → small boundary proposal
     ↑                                             ↓
     └──────── acceptance / resistance / override ┘
```

**Say:** the agent does not diagnose emotion. It observes spatial behavior, acts cautiously, and treats refusal as feedback.

## Slide 3 — Why Power Mesh? (1:30–2:30)

**Show:** the real sample, plus four photographs: relaxed, stretched, layered, and point-pulled. Add one chain only: **tension → aperture/curvature → exposure/access → possible response**.  
**Say:** Power Mesh is a leading candidate because tension is controllable, deformation is continuous, and direct override may be possible. These are hypotheses, not findings.

## Slide 4 — Three precedents and the gap (2:30–3:40)

Use a three-column matrix, one contribution per precedent:

| Precedent | What it contributes | What remains open for this thesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hylozoic Ground | Lightweight textile matrix and local responsive action | Spatial negotiation and long-term learning |
| Muscle Projects | Movement-to-structural transformation | Legibility, refusal, and uncertainty |
| Adaptive Architectural Layout | Full-scale partition, shared control, five-week deployment | Continuous soft deformation and trajectory-based memory |

**Say:** the project combines material responsiveness, movement input, and shared control; the gap is a boundary that learns cautiously and can be rejected.

## Slide 5 — What I will test next (3:40–5:00)

**Show:** EXP-001 → EXP-002, with Open / Guide / Enclose sketches.

- EXP-001: characterize stretch, layering, anchors, recovery, and failure.
- EXP-002: manually simulate the agent and compare fixed, manual, and proposed configurations.
- Record path, hesitation, touch/override, legibility, and failure.

**End with:** “My next decision is not whether to add motors. It is whether this material can produce repeatable, legible, and negotiable spatial differences.”

## Visual and speaking rules

- Maximum 12–18 visible words per slide, excluding small citations.
- Use one diagram or image family per slide; avoid paragraphs.
- Put references in a small footer and keep a backup bibliography slide.
- Bring one labelled sample and, if available, one failed sample.
- Rehearse to 4:30, leaving 30 seconds for interruption.
- Do not claim privacy, comfort, learning, or preserved agency until tested.
