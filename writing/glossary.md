# Working Glossary

These are cautious **working definitions** for this thesis repository. They are not presented as universally settled terminology, nor do they substitute for later source-based definitions. Each definition may be revised as the research documents literature, design decisions, and evidence.

## Agentic architecture

A provisional term for architecture in which a spatial system has some capacity to observe, select among actions, act, receive feedback, and revise later behavior. In this thesis, the term does not imply independent intention, human-like understanding, or unlimited autonomy.

## Adaptive boundary

A spatial element or assembly that can change conditions such as passage, visibility, enclosure, proximity, or connection in response to observations or occupant input. It is broader than a wall and does not commit the thesis to a curtain, a specific material, or autonomous control.

## Affordance

A context-dependent possibility for action or relation that emerges between an occupant and an environment. A designed change may offer or inhibit an affordance, but it does not guarantee that every occupant will perceive, accept, or use it as anticipated.

## Trajectory

A time-based description of spatial behavior that may include movement, distance, orientation, hesitation, dwell, and route choice. It is treated as an uncertain observable pattern, not as direct evidence of intent or preference.

## Proxemics

A working lens for examining how interpersonal distances, orientations, and spatial arrangements relate to social interaction. The thesis does not assume that any distance has a universal meaning; context, culture, ability, activity, and individual preference may alter its interpretation.

## Agency negotiation

An iterative process through which occupants can influence, accept, interrupt, refuse, reverse, redirect, or reinterpret an adaptive system's actions. The term emphasizes practical capacity to affect outcomes rather than visible compliance alone.

## Co-adaptation

A reciprocal process in which both occupant behavior and system behavior may change over repeated interaction. Demonstrating co-adaptation would require documented change and feedback in both directions; a one-time automated response is not sufficient by definition.

## Architectural agency

The limited capacity attributed to an architectural system to participate in shaping spatial conditions through observation and action. This is a working analytical term and does not equate the system's capacity with human moral, political, or legal agency.

## Non-coercive adaptation

A provisional design aim in which adaptive changes preserve meaningful opportunities for occupants to understand, ignore, interrupt, refuse, reverse, contest, or appropriate system actions. It is not assumed to be achieved merely because an action is soft, reversible, or technically optional; its criteria require explicit evaluation.
