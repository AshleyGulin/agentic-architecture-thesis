## Primary question

**[Q0](../questions/question-tree.md#q0):** How can an adaptive architectural boundary learn from occupants' spatial behavior and alter spatial affordances while preserving occupants' capacity to negotiate, resist, or reinterpret its actions?

## Operational terms

These are cautious working definitions for question formation; the fuller [glossary](glossary.md) records their provisional status.

- **Adaptive architectural boundary:** a spatial element or assembly that can change conditions such as passage, visibility, enclosure, proximity, or connection in response to observations or input.
- **Learn from:** revise an action tendency or interpretation over time using documented feedback; this does not imply direct access to occupant intent.
- **Spatial behavior:** observable movement and relation to people, objects, routes, and boundaries within a defined setting.
- **Trajectory:** a time-based record that may include movement, distance, orientation, hesitation, dwell, and route choice, together with uncertainty and context.
- **Spatial affordance:** a context-dependent possibility for action or relation that the environment offers or inhibits; it is not guaranteed to be perceived or used as intended.
- **Negotiation:** an iterative exchange in which occupant action can alter, refuse, reverse, redirect, or appropriate a boundary action.
- **Resistance:** an occupant response that declines, interrupts, avoids, reverses, or works around an adaptive action; it should not automatically be classified as error.
- **Reinterpretation:** use or response that differs from the system's apparent expectation and may reveal another viable spatial meaning.
- **Preserving capacity:** maintaining practical and legible opportunities for occupant choice, including non-action, manual action, refusal, recovery, and contestation.

## Explicit revision criteria

Revise the primary or dependent wording when one or more of the following is documented:

1. **Context failure:** comparison shows that the compact domestic context does not produce a meaningful adaptive-boundary problem or requires a materially different social scenario.
2. **Observability failure:** candidate trajectory dimensions cannot be operationalized responsibly or do not distinguish relevant spatial behavior from ambiguity.
3. **Action-space failure:** proposed boundary changes do not produce architecturally meaningful or safe affordance differences.
4. **Agency failure:** the interaction model cannot provide credible opportunities for refusal, reversal, reinterpretation, or recovery.
5. **Contribution failure:** evaluation cannot distinguish the work from manual adjustment, one-way optimization, or technical mechanism performance.
6. **Scope or ethics trigger:** a proposed revision materially changes the population, domestic scenario, sensing method, privacy risk, or central contribution and therefore requires student approval before adoption.

When revision is triggered, retain the stable question ID, document the prior wording and rationale, update linked claim rows, and keep unsupported interpretations labeled as Hypotheses.
