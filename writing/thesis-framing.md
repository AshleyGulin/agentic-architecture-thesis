# Provisional Thesis Framing

## Status

This document is a provisional framing Hypothesis. It does not state supported findings. Its terms, scenario, materialization, and proposed contribution remain open to revision through traceable sources, documented design work, and evidence.

## Problem

Compact domestic environments may need to support privacy, access, attention, passage, and togetherness as activities and social relations change. A fixed spatial arrangement may not serve every such condition equally, while an automated arrangement could reduce rather than expand occupant agency if it treats inferred intent as a command. The research problem is therefore not simply how to move a boundary, but how an adaptive architectural boundary might alter spatial affordances without over-controlling the people who live with it.

## Context

The candidate context is a compact domestic environment with a primary occupant and changing social presence, such as a guest, collaborator, caregiver, or other temporarily present person. This scenario is provisional and does not commit the thesis to a household type, identity, or social script. The architectural primitive is an adaptive boundary rather than a wall alone: it may change degrees of passage, visibility, enclosure, proximity, or connection. A curtain or another soft, continuously adjustable element is a candidate materialization, not a selected mechanism.

The candidate behavioral signal is trajectory understood as a temporal pattern that may include movement, distance, orientation, hesitation, dwell, and route choice. These dimensions are not presumed to reveal intent. Their observability, ethical acceptability, interpretive limits, and relevance to spatial negotiation require testing.

## Gap

The repository does not yet document literature, precedents, observations, or experiments that establish when adaptive boundaries are needed, which trajectory features are meaningful, how occupants negotiate with an adaptive system, or how architectural value should be evaluated. The working gap is thus a research task, not a field-wide claim: the thesis needs an evidence-grounded account connecting sensed spatial behavior, boundary action, occupant response, and architectural consequence.

## Proposition

**Hypothesis:** an adaptive architectural boundary could participate in human-architecture co-adaptation by using limited and uncertain readings of spatial behavior to propose reversible changes in affordance while preserving occupants' capacity to anticipate, negotiate, resist, reverse, or reinterpret those changes.

This proposition does not assume that autonomous action is always desirable. Manual control, non-action, and fixed arrangements remain necessary comparison conditions.

## Intended contribution

The intended contribution is a provisional architectural framework linking:

1. observable but uncertain trajectory dimensions;
2. a bounded vocabulary of adaptive-boundary actions;
3. explicit opportunities for occupant negotiation and refusal; and
4. evaluation of changing spatial relations over time.

The contribution would need to be demonstrated through spatial and experiential consequences, not claimed from the technical performance of a moving mechanism alone.

## Scope

- A provisional compact domestic context with a primary occupant and changing social presence.
- An adaptive boundary that can alter spatial affordances; no single material or mechanism is selected.
- Trajectory dimensions including movement, distance, orientation, hesitation, dwell, and route choice.
- Interaction over time among observation, boundary action, occupant response, and system revision.
- Architectural and agency-centered evaluation, including ambiguity, refusal, failure, and recovery.

## Non-goals

- Proving that trajectory directly reveals intent.
- Optimizing occupant behavior toward a single preferred pattern.
- Committing to a curtain, sensor, control policy, or household scenario before comparison.
- Treating automation, mechanical novelty, or prediction accuracy as sufficient architectural contribution.
- Claiming universal applicability beyond the context and evidence eventually documented.

## Assumptions to test

- **Hypothesis linked to [Q1](../questions/question-tree.md#q1) and [C1](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c1):** changing domestic relations create a meaningful need for an adaptive boundary.
- **Hypothesis linked to [Q2](../questions/question-tree.md#q2) and [C2](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c2):** selected trajectory dimensions can provide useful signals without being treated as direct evidence of intent.
- **Hypothesis linked to [Q3](../questions/question-tree.md#q3) and [C3](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c3):** gradated, reversible boundary actions can create negotiable changes in spatial affordance.
- **Risk hypothesis linked to [Q4](../questions/question-tree.md#q4) and [C4](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c4):** legibility, interruption, reversal, and reinterpretation can help limit agent over-control.
- **Contribution hypothesis linked to [Q5](../questions/question-tree.md#q5) and [C5](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c5):** evaluation can distinguish architectural co-adaptation from a technically responsive moving mechanism.
