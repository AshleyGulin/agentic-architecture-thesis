# Provisional Adaptive-Boundary Action Space

## Status

No boundary material, mechanism, geometry, actuator, or action vocabulary has been selected. A curtain is one candidate within a rigid-to-soft comparison, not the thesis object by default. This document defines Hypotheses and Open questions for [Q3](../questions/question-tree.md#q3) and [C3](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c3), with agency and safety links to [Q4](../questions/question-tree.md#q4) and [C4](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c4).

## Candidate boundary families

| Candidate family | Possible architectural operation | Potential strengths to test | Risks and Limitations to test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed baseline | Holds passage, visibility, and enclosure constant. | Establishes whether change is needed at all. | May not accommodate changing relations; that insufficiency is not yet documented. |
| Rigid sliding or hinged panel | Changes opening, alignment, separation, or connection through discrete or continuous movement. | Clear geometry and potentially legible states. | Pinch/crush zones, blockage, mass, noise, binary behavior, and egress constraints. |
| Folding or articulated semi-rigid assembly | Changes extent, curvature, thickness, or enclosure. | Multiple configurations and compact storage may be possible. | Complex kinematics, entrapment, maintenance, and unpredictable swept volume. |
| Soft textile boundary, including a curtain | Changes position, drape, overlap, curvature, porosity, opacity, height, or tension. | Gradated and potentially reversible spatial effects. | Entanglement, snagging, unstable geometry, cleaning, acoustic limits, accessibility, and false assumptions that softness ensures agency. |
| Pneumatic, inflatable, or materially variable boundary | Changes volume, stiffness, porosity, or enclosure. | Broad transformation may be possible. | Pressure, puncture, noise, maintenance, slow recovery, and unfamiliar failure states. |

These are design-study categories, not claims that every member can perform the listed operations.

## Candidate action dimensions

| Dimension | Candidate representation | Architectural effect to test | Safety or agency constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Position | Coordinate, track percentage, or named zone | Shifts division, access, adjacency, or territorial boundary. | Preserve egress and accessible routes; show destination before movement where possible. |
| Opening width | Length or percentage of maximum clear opening | Changes passage, connection, and potential visibility. | Maintain approved minimum clearance; never close on an occupied path. |
| Curvature | Radius, control points, or qualitative geometry state | Redirects route, shapes enclosure, or changes proximity. | Bound swept area and avoid trapping or hidden pinch zones. |
| Porosity/opacity | Measured material state or calibrated discrete level | Changes visual connection, screening, airflow, or light. | Do not equate visibility with consent, attention, or privacy preference. |
| Height | Length or percentage of travel | Changes enclosure, sightline, and connection above or below the boundary. | Bound overhead and floor-level hazards; preserve reach and accessibility. |
| Tension | Force, extension, or calibrated state | Alters stability, drape, acoustic behavior, or tactile response. | Set conservative force limits and release safely under obstruction or manual intervention. |
| Response speed | Linear/angular rate and acceleration profile | Changes immediacy, legibility, and time available to respond. | Use bounded speed/acceleration, warning or proposal states, and immediate stop behavior. |

## Provisional action protocol

1. **Proposal or preview:** communicate the candidate action, reason, uncertainty, affected zone, and how to refuse it.
2. **Consent gate:** require explicit initiation or confirmation for untested, privacy-changing, route-changing, multi-occupant, or higher-risk actions.
3. **Bounded motion:** move only within verified mechanical, clearance, force, and speed limits while monitoring the action zone.
4. **Occupant override:** provide a reachable, legible stop; accept verbal, physical, interface, or other approved override channels; do not classify refusal as error.
5. **Safe stop and reversal:** on uncertainty, sensing loss, obstruction, conflict, or override, stop safely and allow manual recovery or reversal.
6. **Feedback:** record only consented, minimized interaction data and distinguish the observed response from any Interpretation.

## Comparison and Evidence needed

For each candidate, compare fixed, direct manual, agent-proposed, and—if ethically and technically approved—bounded automatic conditions. Document spatial affordance, occupant control, reversibility, accessibility, failure modes, maintenance, energy, and transfer risk. Mechanical performance or softness alone is not Evidence of architectural value or preserved agency.
