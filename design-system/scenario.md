# Provisional Scenario Comparison

## Status

These compact domestic scenarios are comparison prompts, not selected contexts, observed households, or Evidence. Each assumes a primary occupant and changing social presence only to expose candidate spatial conflicts. No identity, relationship, activity, or need should be treated as universal.

Related inquiry: [Q1](../questions/question-tree.md#q1), [Q4](../questions/question-tree.md#q4), and [Q5](../questions/question-tree.md#q5); [C1](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c1), [C4](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c4), and [C5](../claims-and-evidence/evidence-matrix.md#c5).

## Shared variables to document

- **Social presence:** count, role as described by participants, arrival/departure, and whether each person has consented to sensing and adaptation.
- **Activities:** participant-described activity rather than intent inferred from trajectory alone.
- **Spatial relations:** passage, visibility, acoustic separation, proximity, connection, access, and enclosure.
- **Privacy and territoriality:** desired separation, control rights, competing claims, retreat, and bystander exposure.
- **Circulation:** accessible route, width, clearance, crossings, detours, egress, and proximity to the moving boundary.
- **Control condition:** fixed layout, direct manual reconfiguration, agent-proposed change, and—only if approved—bounded automatic change.
- **Occupant agency:** anticipation, consent, non-action, interruption, reversal, redirection, reinterpretation, and recovery.

## Selection criteria

Score or describe each candidate only after traceable Source review and documented design study or observation.

1. A changing spatial relation is clear and architecturally consequential.
2. Fixed and manual alternatives can be compared fairly.
3. The scenario exposes meaningful agency questions rather than manufacturing a need for automation.
4. Sensing and data collection could be proportionate, consent-based, and privacy-preserving.
5. Boundary action could remain reversible, safe, accessible, and easy to override.
6. Spatial and experiential outcomes could be evaluated without treating inferred intent as fact.
7. The scope is feasible and does not presume vulnerable participants or high-risk activity without ethics review.

## Decision rule

Do not select a scenario from this comparison alone. A consequential narrowing is a Decision requiring rationale, supporting Evidence, consequences, a reconsideration trigger, and student approval under the [agent contract](../AGENTS.md).
